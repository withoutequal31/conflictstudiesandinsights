01

Russia–Ukraine

Infrastructure strikes increase pressure ahead of winter. Operational dynamics across the frontline reflected shifting momentum as Ukrainian forces contested Russian positions, leading to localised Russian setbacks in key operational sectors. In response, Russian forces intensified targeted strike campaigns against critical infrastructure, causing widespread energy grid disruptions, blackouts and water shortages in Kyiv. Simultaneously, high-altitude and long-range strike operations continued to surge, marked by large-scale Russian drone and missile salvos alongside deep Ukrainian strikes targeting defence-industrial infrastructure and oil refineries within Russian territory. Source: The Guardian, 9 Nov

02

Russia–Europe tensions

Escalating hybrid warfare, border fortifications and diplomatic impasses. Tensions escalated significantly as European intelligence agencies attributed a wave of hybrid intelligence operations, including widespread bomb hoaxes and aviation disruption, directly to Russian state actors. In response to rapid Russian rearmament and strategic force restructuring along NATO’s eastern flank, alliance members actively evaluated deploying physical anti-tank barriers, and the United Kingdom announced autonomous maritime deployments to counter Russian interference in sovereign waters. Furthermore, Moscow firmly rejected European participation in broader post-conflict security architecture discussions, reiterating its opposition to NATO integration and any foreign security guarantees for Kyiv. Source: ISW, 7 Nov

03

US–Israel–Iran

Limited strikes and reported contingency plans sustain escalation concerns. Following the military escalation that disrupted Iranian defence infrastructure and command nodes, the overall theatre remains in a fragile, highly monitored posture under third-party diplomatic mediation. While conditional ceasefire terms and international pressure have curbed large-scale direct state-on-state kinetic exchanges, covert operations and proxy engagements persist. Both Washington and Tel Aviv maintain high defensive readiness and enforcement postures to prevent Tehran from reconstituting its long-range missile and nuclear capabilities.

04

Israel–Lebanon

High-intensity border interdiction, infrastructure demolitions and mass displacement. Kinetic engagements along the northern border remain high-intensity, characterised by targeted airstrikes, localised ground operations and artillery exchanges across southern Lebanon. The Israel Defense Forces continue enforcing operational access control up to key tactical boundaries, aiming to suppress cross-border rocket networks and dismantle Hezbollah infrastructure. These ongoing operations have resulted in extensive destruction of residential structures in southern border communities and continuous civilian displacement across the Litani River sector. Source: Al Jazeera, 1 Nov

05

Israel–Gaza

Urban kinetic operations, demolition campaigns and humanitarian access blockades. The conflict across the Gaza Strip maintains a severe kinetic and humanitarian baseline, marked by ongoing localised airstrikes, artillery bombardments and structural demolitions in urban sectors. Strict operational enforcement and legal restrictions by Israeli authorities continue to prevent international humanitarian agencies, including UNRWA, from deploying personnel or directly distributing critical aid stockpiles. This operational friction, combined with seasonal weather impacts on temporary displacement camps, has left over half the enclave’s population exposed to severe shelter and resource deficits.

Intelligence / Protection

A warning is not yet a protective response

Russia–Europe tensions, Black Sea · 27 Oct – 9 Nov 2026

Preliminary One Reuters report, quoting the French commander. The detection and the one-hour warning have not been independently verified.

LessonA warning protects only if someone can act on it in time. Here it saved the crew but not the ship, so count personnel, assets and mission continuity as separate outcomes.

Questions to askWhen another unit’s sensor warns us of a threat, can we turn that into an authorised protective action before impact, and what would that action actually save?

Learning status
Preliminary observation. The incident highlights a potential gap between warning and effective protection; the underlying constraints and applicability to Army operations require further validation.
QuestionPreliminary findings
Operating problemDetection and communication may work while the wider response chain lacks an available effector, a clear decision owner or sufficient response time. The reporting establishes the surveillance aircraft’s inability to intercept; it does not establish which other constraints determined the outcome.
External observationOn 28 October, surveillance detected sea drones approximately one hour before they struck the Contship Jet. Warning contributed to crew survival, but did not prevent the vessel’s loss. Source: Reuters, 5 Nov
CountermeasureFrance deployed an Atlantique 2 maritime patrol aircraft to Romania to strengthen surveillance and detect threats to commercial shipping. During the 28 October incident, it detected three sea drones and alerted Bulgarian authorities and the Contship Jet approximately one hour before impact. The commander said this warning enabled the crew to escape unharmed. France subsequently extended the deployment by several weeks.
Reported limitationsThe aircraft could detect and warn, but could not neutralise or intercept the sea drones. Despite the warning, the drones struck and sank the vessel. The reported protective effect was therefore crew survival, not prevention of the attack or preservation of the ship.
What remains unprovenThe report does not explain whether another interception asset was available, what action Bulgarian authorities took after receiving the warning, or whether response authority, coordination or timing constrained intervention.
Preliminary observations

Warning can protect personnel without preserving the asset. The reported advance warning enabled the Contship Jet crew to escape, but the vessel was still struck and sunk. Personnel survival, asset protection and mission continuity should therefore be assessed as separate outcomes.

Validation question

When an external sensor provides warning of an approaching threat, can the receiving unit translate it into an authorised, executable protective action before impact — and what outcome can that action realistically preserve: personnel, assets or mission continuity?

Sustainment / Engineers

Separate backups can share one point of failure

Russia–Ukraine · 27 Oct – 9 Nov 2026

Preliminary Reuters reported the strikes and outages from Ukrainian authorities. The interception figures are Ukrainian and not independently verified, and no report says whether backups failed.

Swipe for the next panel →

LessonHaving backups is not the same as being resilient. What matters is whether they still work when a shared utility or access route is cut.

Questions to askIf a shared power line or road is cut, can our backup arrangements keep essential functions running on their own, or do several fail together?

Russia–Ukraine

Function
Sustainment, with links to engineers
Learning status
Preliminary observation, requires validation. The reported disruption to several essential services supports examining shared infrastructure dependencies. However, the available evidence does not establish which backup arrangements existed, whether they failed, or why. The proposition that separate backups may share a common vulnerability remains a question for validation, not a demonstrated finding from this incident.
QuestionPreliminary findings
Specific observationDuring the 6–7 November strikes, Russia damaged electricity transmission infrastructure supplying Kyiv and central Ukraine, disrupting electricity, water and heating as temperatures approached freezing. The attack also damaged Kyiv’s Northern Bridge. The case illustrates how damage to upstream infrastructure and transport connections can affect several functions during the same attack. Source: Reuters, 7 Nov
CountermeasureUkraine employed air defences against the incoming missiles and drones. Ukrainian authorities reported intercepting four of 36 ballistic missiles and 149 of 172 drones; these are Ukrainian figures, not independently verified results.
Reported limitationsDespite the reported interceptions, strikes damaged electricity transmission infrastructure and disrupted several essential services. Damage to the Northern Bridge added a separate transport disruption. The reported interception totals therefore did not translate into uninterrupted infrastructure operation.
What remains unprovenThe preliminary implication is that resilience depends not simply on having backup systems, but on whether those alternatives remain usable when shared utilities or access routes are disrupted; the incident does not establish that Ukraine’s backups shared these vulnerabilities or failed.
Preliminary observations

The strikes suggest that damage to upstream electricity connections can disrupt several essential services simultaneously, even when generating facilities remain available. Concurrent damage to power infrastructure and a major bridge also raises the possibility of competing demands on restoration and movement, although the reporting does not establish that bridge damage delayed repairs.

Validation question

If a shared utility or access route is disrupted, can the formation’s backup arrangements sustain essential functions independently — or do several alternatives become unavailable together?
